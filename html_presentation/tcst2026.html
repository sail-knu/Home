Distance constraints in MPC (MPC-DC)

MPC-DC
\(i=0,\ldots,N-1\)

Distance constraints need a long horizon

Horizon 1.5 sstuck at the obstacle
Horizon 3.0 sreaches the goal

Obstacle avoidance constraints are only activated when the vehicle is close to obstacles

Background · CBF-based MPC [1]

CBF constraints in MPC (MPC-CBF)

MPC-CBF same cost and constraints as MPC-DC, except the obstacle constraint
\(\text{s.t.}\) \(\mathbf{x}_0-\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{init}}=0,\ \ \mathbf{x}_{i+1}-f_d(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{u}_i)=0,\ \ \mathbf{x}_i\in\mathbf{X},\ \mathbf{u}_i\in\mathbf{U}\) \(\Delta h_e(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{u}_i)+\alpha_e h_e(\mathbf{x}_i)\ge 0\) Discrete-time CBFreplaces \(h(\mathbf{x}_i)\ge 0\) \(\Delta h_e(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{u}_i)=h_e(\mathbf{x}_{i+1})-h_e(\mathbf{x}_i)\) One-step change \(h_e(\mathbf{x})=\dot h(\mathbf{x})+\alpha\,h(\mathbf{x})\ge 0,\ \ \alpha>0\) Exponential CBFof the distance \(h\)
  • The CBF limits the speed toward an obstacle in proportion to the relative distance from it
  • MPC-CBF with a shorter horizon can still achieve performance similar to MPC-DC [1]
[1] J. Zeng, B. Zhang, and K. Sreenath, “Safety-critical model predictive control with discrete-time control barrier function,” ACC 2021 04
Motivation · Euclidean distance-based CBF (ED-CBF)

ED-CBF overlooks heading and turning

1

Only the closest Euclidean distance

Conventional CBFs measure the shortest distance to the obstacle — the ED-CBF

2

Heading and turning are ignored

Limited turning capability is a common characteristic of nonholonomic vehicles — cars, robots, ships

3

Safe states are perceived as risks

States that are actually safe for navigation are restricted → conservative trajectories

Proposed

Nonholonomic characteristics in the CBF design → Turning Circle-based CBF (TC-CBF)

05
Proposed method

Turning Circle-based CBF (TC-CBF)

Clearance is measured from the vehicle's left and right turning circles

1
Two turning circles from the speed \(u\) and max. turning rate \(r_{\max}\)
\(R=u\,/\,r_{\max},\qquad \mathbf{p}_{tl},\,\mathbf{p}_{tr}=\mathbf{p}\pm R\,\big[\cos(\psi+\tfrac{\pi}{2}),\,\sin(\psi+\tfrac{\pi}{2})\big]^{\top}\)
2
A CBF for each circle: circle-to-obstacle clearance
\[\begin{aligned}h_{tl}(\mathbf{x})&=\big\|\mathbf{p}_{tl}-\mathbf{o}\big\|-(o_r+R+R_s)\\ h_{tr}(\mathbf{x})&=\big\|\mathbf{p}_{tr}-\mathbf{o}\big\|-(o_r+R+R_s)\end{aligned}\]
3
Safe while at least one turning circle does not intersect the obstacle
\(h_t(\mathbf{x})=\max\big(h_{tr}(\mathbf{x}),\,h_{tl}(\mathbf{x})\big)\)
implemented with a smooth log-sum-exp approximation of the max
  • Always leaves space on the left or right to avoid obstacles
  • In MPC as a discrete-time CBF: \(\Delta h_t(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{u}_i)+\alpha_t\,h_t(\mathbf{x}_i)\ge 0\)

Conclusion

TC-CBF for efficient collision avoidance

01

Turning circles in the CBF

The CBF is defined by the clearance between the obstacle and the vehicle's left and right turning circles, so heading and limited turning capability enter the safety constraint.

02

Less conservative, still safe

Only states from which neither turn avoids the obstacle are restricted — MPC-TCCBF avoids unnecessary slowdowns and aggressive turns with a short horizon.

03

Validated on a real ASV

Unicycle simulations and Heron ASV experiments: vs MPC-EDCBF, arrival time 10%↓, speed error 56%↓, cross-track error 17%↓ on average.
